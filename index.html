02 — understand

Eating disorders can look like anything

They affect people of every age, gender, body size, race and background. You don't have to "look sick" to deserve help.

🌙

Anorexia

Restricting food, intense fear of weight gain, and a distorted view of one's body — at any body size.

🌊

Bulimia

Cycles of eating large amounts and then trying to compensate, often hidden behind shame and secrecy.

🍂

Binge eating disorder

Repeated episodes of eating with a feeling of loss of control, often followed by distress. The most common ED.

🌿

ARFID

Avoiding or restricting foods because of sensory issues, fear of choking or low interest — not body image.

✨

OSFED

Serious eating disorders that don't fit one neat category. Just as real, just as deserving of treatment.

💭

Warning signs

Preoccupation with food or body, skipping meals with others, rigid rituals, mood changes, withdrawing from people.

05 — show up

Supporting someone you love

Start with care, not food

Pick a calm, private moment. Use "I" statements: "I've noticed you seem stressed around meals, and I care about you." Focus on feelings and behaviour you've seen, not weight or appearance.

Expect resistance — and stay

Denial, anger or secrecy are common. That doesn't mean you failed. Let them know the door stays open and you're not going anywhere.

Avoid body and diet talk

Skip comments on anyone's size, "good" or "bad" foods, and compliments about weight loss. Praise who they are, not how they look.

Encourage professional help

Offer to help find a doctor, therapist or dietitian experienced in eating disorders — or to sit with them while they call a helpline.

Look after yourself too

Supporting someone is heavy. Carers' groups like F.E.A.S.T. and Beat's carer services exist for you, too.
